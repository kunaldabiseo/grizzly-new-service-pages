The short answer

How to insulate an older factory building

Test before anything is disturbed, because older factories can hold asbestos and lead paint. Then read the walls: solid brick, hollow block and concrete each handle moisture differently. Pick the roof approach by what the deck is made of and when the roof is due, and only then insulate, air-seal and finish to the code that applies to the building's use.

Walls

Insulating brick, block and concrete factory walls

Old masonry walls dry by letting heat and air pass through them. Insulate the inside and the outer brick gets colder and wetter in winter, which can lead to freeze-thaw damage. That is why the wall type, its condition and how much rain it takes all decide the method.

Interior insulation, after a masonry check

Closed-cell spray foam or rigid board tight to the brick, then a stud wall and a finish. Foam tight to the masonry keeps warm indoor air off the cold brick. Thickness is chosen with the freeze-thaw risk in mind.

Watch forBrick that takes a lot of rain, failing mortar, missing gutters and embedded steel. These get fixed or assessed before the wall is closed in.

Roofs and glass

Sawtooth roofs, old decks and factory windows

Older factories were built for daylight, with sawtooth and monitor roofs and walls of steel windows. That light is worth keeping. The insulation plan works around it and is timed with the roof.

Wood plank decks

Common in the oldest buildings. Insulation usually goes above the deck when the roof is replaced, so the old timber stays warm and dry. Insulating under a wood deck needs a moisture check first.

Concrete decks

Heavy and durable. Rigid insulation above the deck at reroof is the usual route. From below, spray foam or board is possible where the ceiling height allows.

Metal decks

Found in later additions. Closed-cell spray foam or a liner system from below, or rigid insulation above at reroof.

Sawtooth glass and steel windows

Insulation cannot fix single glazing. We insulate and seal the frames, curbs and walls around the glass, and leave the window choice to your design team.

Test first

Asbestos, lead and old insulation: test before anything comes down

Older factories often have asbestos in pipe wrap, roofing, flooring or old insulation, and lead in paint. In Colorado, commercial renovation that disturbs more than the trigger amounts needs an inspection by a state-certified asbestos building inspector first.

  1. InspectionA Colorado-certified asbestos building inspector samples what the job will disturb.
  2. Abatement if neededRegulated material is removed by a certified abatement contractor, with state notice.
  3. Old insulation outGrizzly removes clean, non-asbestos insulation that is wet, oily, soiled or failing.
  4. New insulation inOnly then does the new system go up.

Questions

Factory insulation contractor FAQs

What does a factory insulation contractor do?

A factory insulation contractor insulates and air-seals the roof, walls and interior spaces of factory buildings. For older factories that also means working around test results for asbestos and lead, choosing wall methods that suit old brick, block or concrete, and timing roof insulation with a reroof. Grizzly also removes old, non-asbestos insulation before new insulation goes in.

Can you insulate old brick factory walls from the inside?

Sometimes, after a check. Insulating solid brick from the inside makes the outer brick colder and slower to dry, which raises the risk of freeze-thaw damage. Building science research recommends assessing the masonry, water exposure and embedded steel first. Many owners start with the roof and air sealing and handle brick walls once they have been assessed.

Do you remove asbestos?

No. Asbestos must be identified by a Colorado-certified asbestos building inspector, and regulated material is removed by a certified abatement contractor. We remove clean, non-asbestos insulation and install the new system once testing and any abatement are done.

Does an old factory need an asbestos inspection before insulation work?

If the work will disturb more than Colorado's trigger amounts in a commercial building (260 linear feet of pipe, 160 square feet of other surfaces or a 55-gallon drum's worth), Regulation 8 requires an inspection by a certified inspector first. Buildings put up after 12 Oct 1988 may qualify for an exception with documentation.

How do you insulate concrete block walls?

Two common ways: inject foam into the hollow cores, which keeps the wall's look and adds some insulation, or insulate the inside face with rigid board or spray foam and add a finish, which adds much more. We check for cracks and moisture before choosing.

What changes when a factory becomes offices or lofts?

A change of use that raises energy demand is generally held to the current energy code, and residential units bring fire separation and sound rating rules between units. Insulation is planned with your architect so it fits those assemblies. Designated historic buildings can be exempt from parts of the energy code when a report is filed.

Should we insulate the roof now or wait for a reroof?

If the roof has only a few years left, insulation above the deck at reroof is often the better value, and replacing a roof with insulation above the deck generally means bringing it up to current values. If the roof has many years left, insulating from below now may make more sense. We look at both on the walk-through.

How much does factory insulation cost?

Factory insulation cost depends on what has to come out first, test results, wall type and condition, roof timing, the planned use and access. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
