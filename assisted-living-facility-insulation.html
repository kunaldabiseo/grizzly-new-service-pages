What an assisted living facility insulation contractor does

An assisted living facility insulation contractor insulates and seals the walls, floors, attics and roofs of a senior living community, so apartments are quieter and more comfortable and common areas don't disturb the people living next to them. It works around residents and leaves heating units, windows and fire protection to the trades that own them.

The short answer

How to insulate an assisted living facility

Start where residents live: fill the walls between apartments and to the corridor, and the floor and ceiling cavities between levels, and seal the gaps. Then wall off the noisy rooms, like dining, kitchen, laundry and activities, from nearby apartments. Air seal and insulate the attic and the outside walls of cold apartments. Plan the work around residents, using empty apartments between move-ins whenever you can.

Questions

Assisted living facility insulation contractor FAQs

What does an assisted living facility insulation contractor do?

An assisted living facility insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals the walls, floors, attics and roofs of a senior living community so apartments are quieter and more comfortable and busy common rooms don't disturb residents. Heating units, windows, fire protection and finishes come from other trades.

Residents can hear their neighbors. What helps?

Filling the shared wall, sealing back-to-back outlets and gaps at the floor and making sure the wall runs fully to the floor and ceiling above. For noise from upstairs, cavity insulation helps, and the floor finish and underlayment do much of the work.

What sound rating is required between apartments?

The International Building Code sets a minimum of STC 50 for walls and floors between dwelling and sleeping units and adjacent public areas, and IIC 50 for floor and ceiling assemblies. Your local adopted code governs, and older buildings were not always built to it.

Can quieter walls help in memory care?

The Alzheimer's Association lists noise and background distraction as possible triggers for agitation. Insulating bedroom and corridor walls and walling off busy shared rooms reduces noise; care routines, lighting and finishes do the rest.

Why are some apartments colder than others?

Corner and end apartments with two outside walls, top-floor apartments under a thin attic and apartments over unheated spaces lose more heat. Insulating and air sealing those walls and the attic evens them out. One apartment that is always different is often a heating unit issue.

Do we have to move residents out?

No. We prefer to work in vacant apartments between move-out and move-in, and in occupied wings we keep noisy work to agreed daytime hours, keep corridors clear and clean up every day.

Can you insulate the attic over our top floor?

Yes. We air seal the holes from the apartments into the attic first, keep eave vents open, then blow in insulation to the planned depth, leaving sprinkler piping, draft stops and fire separations as designed.

Do you work in memory care neighborhoods?

Yes, with your care team. We agree on hours and routes, keep tools and materials secured at all times and keep the work area closed off from residents.

How much does assisted living facility insulation cost?

Assisted living facility insulation cost depends on the number of apartments and whether they are vacant, which walls or floors need work, attic area and access, common rooms, scheduling around residents and any removal. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
