The short answer

How to insulate a cold storage facility

Cold storage turns normal building rules around. Moisture always moves from the warm outside toward the cold inside, so the vapor retarder goes on the warm side and has to be continuous. Insulation is thicker, every penetration is sealed, freezer floors are protected from frost heave, and doors and docks are planned so warm air does not pour in.

  1. 01

    Warm side, sealed

    A continuous vapor retarder and air seal on the warm side, usually the outside of the insulation.

  2. 02

    Thicker and continuous

    More insulation than a normal building, with no gaps at joints, edges or framing.

  3. 03

    Every hole sealed

    Refrigerant lines, drains and conduit are the usual places ice starts.

  4. 04

    Floors protected

    Freezer floors need insulation and under-slab heating so the ground below does not freeze.

  5. 05

    Doors planned

    Fast doors, vestibules and dock seals keep warm, wet air out of the cold.

Vapor retarder

Why the vapor retarder goes on the warm side

In a normal heated building the warm side is inside. In cold storage it is outside, all year. Put the vapor retarder on the wrong side, or leave gaps in it, and moisture ends up inside the insulation, where it freezes. Try the three options.

Dry insulation

The vapor retarder stops moist air before it gets into the insulation. The insulation stays dry and keeps working. This is the standard approach in cold storage.

Refrigeration load

How the building shell adds to your refrigeration load

Refrigeration removes heat and moisture that get into the cold room. Some comes from product and people. A lot comes through the building: through the shell, through air leaks and through doors. The tighter the envelope, the less the system has to fight.

Heat through the shell

Warm roof decks and walls around the box pass heat inward all day, more in summer. Insulating the building shell keeps the space around the box cooler.

Ours to fix

Air leaks carry moisture

Leaks bring in heat and water vapor. The water freezes on evaporator coils, which then need more defrost cycles, and on surfaces as frost and ice.

Working in live rooms

Can you work while the rooms stay cold?

Often, yes, for work on the shell and the space above the boxes. Work that touches the cold side needs a plan with your refrigeration contractor. These are the three ways it usually goes.

Room keeps running

Outside the box

Roof deck, walls, dock wall and the space above the box are worked from the warm side while the room stays at temperature.

Room partly affected

Protected zone

A sealed work zone at a panel joint or penetration, with short shifts in cold-weather gear and product moved away from the area.

Room emptied

Planned warm-up

For bigger repairs, the room is emptied and warmed up on a plan your refrigeration contractor sets, then pulled back down after.

Fire and code

Foam, thermal barriers and code in cold storage

Cold rooms are full of foam: panel cores, board and sometimes spray foam. The fire rules for foam plastic still apply when the room is cold.

IBC 2603.4

Thermal barrier

Foam plastic insulation in occupied space has to be separated from the interior by a thermal barrier, such as 1/2-inch gypsum board or an approved coating (IBC 2603.4), unless the product's listing and the code allow otherwise. Panels are tested and listed as systems.

Product listing

Listings matter

The exact foam, coating or panel and how it was tested decide what is allowed where. We work from the listing, not from habit.

Engineer sign-off

Refrigerant rooms

Machine rooms for ammonia or other refrigerants have their own safety rules, set by your refrigeration engineer. We do not work inside them without their sign-off.

Cold storage insulation contractor FAQs

Call and talk to the crew that will actually be on your roof deck.

What does a cold storage insulation contractor do?

A cold storage insulation contractor insulates and air-seals the building around coolers and freezers, seals penetrations through cold room panels, removes wet or iced insulation and, where a designer specifies it, installs foam systems with a continuous warm-side seal. At Grizzly we are clear about the line: insulated panel boxes, refrigeration and freezer floors belong to specialists.

Do you build cooler and freezer boxes?

No. Insulated metal panel boxes are built by panel installers and refrigeration contractors. We handle the building shell around them, the space above the box, sealing around penetrations and dock walls, and we work alongside the box builder on conversions.

Can spray foam be used in cold storage?

Sometimes, when a designer specifies it. Closed-cell spray foam has been used on existing building envelopes for coolers, but the warm-side vapor retarder, thickness, thermal barrier and fire listing all have to be designed for the temperature. We install to that design rather than guess it.

Why does ice build up above our freezer?

Usually because warm, moist building air leaks into the space above the panel ceiling or through penetrations and freezes on the cold panels. Sealing that space and the penetrations, and insulating the roof above, cuts off the moisture.

Where does the vapor retarder go in cold storage?

On the warm side of the insulation, which in cold storage is usually the outside, and it has to be continuous. On the cold side, or with gaps, moisture gets into the insulation and freezes.

Why is our freezer floor cracking or lifting?

Usually frost heave: the ground under the freezer freezes and the ice lifts the slab. Freezer floors are built with insulation and under-slab heating to prevent it. That repair belongs to floor and refrigeration specialists, not an insulation contractor.

Can you convert part of our warehouse into a cooler?

We can do our part of it: insulating and sealing the existing building shell and, where your designer specifies it, a foam system with a continuous warm-side seal. The temperatures, loads, floor, doors and refrigeration come from your design team and specialists.

Can you work in an operating cold room?

Often around it rather than inside it. Work on the shell and the space above the box can usually happen while the room runs. Work that touches the cold side needs a plan with your refrigeration contractor, such as a planned warm-up or a protected zone.

How much does cold storage insulation cost?

Cold storage insulation cost depends on the temperature, whether the room is live or empty, removal of wet or iced insulation, the number of penetrations, access and the finish. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
