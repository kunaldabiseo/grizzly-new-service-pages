Straight answer first

What a restaurant insulation contractor does

A restaurant insulation contractor insulates and seals the walls, ceilings and roof of a restaurant: the wall between the kitchen and the dining room, the walls you share with neighbors, the roof over the line and the outside walls around the dining room. That keeps guests comfortable, keeps noise and smells where they belong and takes some load off the HVAC. Hoods, grease ducts, walk-in coolers and HVAC are other trades.

Two kinds of noise

Restaurant noise: what insulation fixes and what it doesn't

Noise bouncing around inside a dining room and noise passing through a wall are two different problems. Insulation in the wall is one of the main fixes for the second and only a small part of the fix for the first. Pick the noise you have.

Echo in the dining room

Hard floors, glass, bare tables and open ceilings bounce sound around the room, so everyone talks louder.

Build-outs and remodels

Insulation that works with your health inspection

In food areas, walls and ceilings have to be smooth, durable and easy to clean, and nonabsorbent where they get wet. Insulation does not meet that on its own, so in a kitchen it goes behind a washable finish. Pick a spot to see what usually applies.

Walk-through noteCovered

Kitchen ceiling

Insulation above a kitchen ceiling sits behind a washable ceiling, such as a vinyl-faced or coated tile or panel your GC installs. Exposed spray foam generally needs a code thermal barrier, such as half-inch gypsum board.

Hoods, grease ducts and their wraps belong to specialist trades.

General guidance, not a code ruling: your health department and building official have the final say. References: FDA Food Code, section 6-101.11 and International Building Code section 2603.4 (thermal barrier over foam plastic).

Restaurants are among the most energy-hungry buildings

In federal survey data, food service buildings use several times more energy per square foot than the average commercial building. Most of it goes into cooking and refrigeration, which insulation does not change. The heating and cooling share is the part a better wall and roof can help with.

Questions

Restaurant insulation contractor FAQs

What does a restaurant insulation contractor do?

A restaurant insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals a restaurant's walls, ceilings and roof: the wall between the kitchen and the dining room, walls shared with neighbors, the roof over the line and the outside walls around the dining room. Hoods, grease ducts, walk-in coolers and HVAC come from other trades.

Will insulating the kitchen wall stop the dining room getting hot?

It slows the heat and noise moving through that wall, which helps the tables beside it. Most kitchen heat leaves through the hood, though, and if the hood pulls more air than is replaced it can pull hot or cold air through the restaurant. That part is for your HVAC contractor.

Can insulation make our dining room quieter?

It depends on the noise. Insulation in walls and ceilings is one of the main fixes for noise passing to neighbors or apartments above. Echo inside the dining room is mostly fixed with sound-absorbing panels and finishes, and insulation is only a small part of that.

Our neighbors can smell our kitchen. Can you help?

Often the shared wall stops at the ceiling or has open holes for pipes and ducts. We fill the wall, take it to the deck and seal the gaps. Smells also depend on exhaust and air pressure, which your HVAC contractor handles.

Can insulation be left exposed in a kitchen?

Generally no. Kitchen walls and ceilings need smooth, durable, easily cleaned surfaces, so insulation goes behind a washable finish, and exposed foam generally needs a code thermal barrier. Your health department and building official have the final say.

Do you insulate hoods, grease ducts or walk-in coolers?

No. Hoods and grease ducts need set clearances and fire-rated wraps from specialist installers, and walk-in coolers are panel systems from refrigeration contractors. We insulate the building around them.

Can you work without closing the restaurant?

Yes. We work after close and stop before prep, cover food and equipment, and leave the space clean for your morning crew. Bigger pieces can be done on a closed day.

When is the best time to insulate a restaurant?

During a build-out or remodel, before walls and ceilings are closed. In an open restaurant, a closed day or the hours after close work well.

How much does restaurant insulation cost?

Restaurant insulation cost depends on which zones need work, access to the walls and ceilings, shared wall length, sound goals, the finishes that close the work and the hours we can work. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
