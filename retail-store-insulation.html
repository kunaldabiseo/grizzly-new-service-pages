What a retail store insulation contractor does

A retail store insulation contractor insulates and seals the roof, exterior walls and shared walls of a store, so the sales floor holds temperature, the back of house is usable and the neighbors stay on their side. The storefront glass, entry doors, air curtains and HVAC belong to other trades, and they often matter as much. Here is the split.

The short answer

How to insulate a retail store

Start with the roof, since it is the biggest surface over the sales floor. Then air seal and insulate the exterior walls and the solid parts around the storefront. Fill the demising walls you share with neighbors and take them to the deck. Insulate the stockroom, office and break room. Leave glass, doors and air curtains to their trades, and do the work overnight or before the store opens.

Where the energy goes

Where a store loses and gains heat

In federal survey data, space heating is the largest single energy use in retail buildings. Heat leaves through the roof, the glass, the doors and the walls, and in summer it comes back in the same way. Pick the season.

  • Through the roof: the biggest surface (ours)
  • Through the storefront glass (glazing contractor)
  • Every time the entry doors open (vestibule or air curtain, door contractor)

Questions

Retail store insulation contractor FAQs

What does a retail store insulation contractor do?

A retail store insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals the roof, exterior walls and shared walls of a store so the sales floor holds temperature, the back of house is usable and neighbors stay on their side. Storefront glass, entry doors, air curtains and HVAC come from other trades.

Why is it cold by our front doors?

Mostly because the doors open all day and the storefront is glass. A vestibule, an air curtain or better glass from door and glazing contractors does the most there. We insulate and seal the solid wall around and above the storefront and the roof over the sales floor.

Where does a store lose the most heat?

Through the roof, the storefront glass, the entry and receiving doors and uninsulated walls. Federal survey data shows space heating is the largest single energy use in retail buildings, so the roof and walls are worth getting right.

Can you insulate the roof over our sales floor while we stay open?

Yes. We work under the deck overnight, cover merchandise and fixtures, move lifts section by section and clean the floor before opening.

We can hear the store next door. What helps?

Filling the demising wall and taking it up to the deck, or closing the space above the ceiling, stops most of it. Impact from gyms and odors from restaurants need fixes on their side too.

Do strip centers use more energy than other retail?

On average, yes. Federal survey data puts enclosed malls and strip centers at about 111 thousand Btu per square foot a year, against about 64 for other retail. Every building is different, so we look at yours.

Who pays for insulation in a leased store?

It depends on the lease. Often the landlord owns the roof and shell and the tenant owns the interior. We work for either and provide paperwork your landlord asks for.

Do you insulate the stockroom and receiving area?

Yes. Roof and exterior wall insulation in the back of house, and the wall between the stockroom and the sales floor. Door seals and dock shelters come from your door contractor.

How much does retail store insulation cost?

Retail store insulation cost depends on roof area and what is up there now, clear height, exterior and demising walls, working hours, protecting merchandise and landlord rules. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
