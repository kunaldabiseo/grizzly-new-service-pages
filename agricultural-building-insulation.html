The short answer

How to insulate an agricultural building

First decide if the building is cold or warm. A cold barn or machine shed usually needs a drip barrier on the roof, not full insulation. A heated shop, a warm livestock barn or produce storage needs insulated walls and roof with a sealed vapor layer. In every case, cover the insulation where animals, birds and rodents can reach it, and plan ventilation with the people who design it.

Cold barn or warm barn

Drip barrier or full insulation?

A cold barn runs close to outside temperature and relies on ventilation. Ontario's agriculture ministry advises covering the roof steel of a cold horse barn with at least R-4 of insulation as a drip barrier. A warm building is a different job. Slide the door to switch between them.

Rodents, birds and livestock

Keeping mice, birds and animals out of the insulation

Exposed insulation in a farm building gets nested in, pecked at and chewed. The answer is not a magic material. It is covering the insulation and closing the ways in. Close each one.

Livestock barns

Moisture, ammonia and washdown in livestock barns

Animals breathe out moisture all day, manure gives off ammonia, and pens get washed down. That combination is hard on insulation facings and fasteners, and it is why ventilation and envelope have to be planned together. Open a stall.

Moisture

Insulation sealed against the roof and walls keeps warm, wet air off cold steel. Ventilation removes the moisture itself.

Ammonia

Ammonia attacks bare steel and thin facings over time. We choose facings, liners and fasteners that hold up, and seal the insulation behind them.

Washdown

Where pens are cleaned with water, finishes have to be washable and insulation fully covered, so it never takes on water.

Ventilation

Fans, inlets and controls are designed and sold by your ag equipment dealer. We build the envelope to match their plan.

Hay and fire

Hay storage: dry roof first, insulation second

Most hay barns do not need full insulation. They need a roof that does not drip and good airflow. And the biggest fire risk in a hay barn is the hay itself: extension safety guidance says moisture content is the main factor that causes hay to spontaneously combust, with bales above 20 percent moisture at risk.

Questions

Agricultural building insulation contractor FAQs

What does an agricultural building insulation contractor do?

An agricultural building insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals farm and ranch buildings: roof drip barriers, post-frame and metal wall insulation, liners that protect insulation from animals and pests, and farm shop offices. Barn fans, heaters, refrigeration and grain systems are installed by other trades.

How do I stop my pole barn roof from dripping?

Keep warm, moist air off the cold roof steel. In an existing barn that usually means closed-cell spray foam under the roof or a liner with insulation. In a new barn it can also be a blanket under the roof steel or factory drip-stop on the panels, ordered from your building supplier.

Does every barn need to be insulated?

No. A cold machine shed or cold barn usually only needs a drip barrier under the roof. Ontario's agriculture ministry suggests at least R-4 on the roof steel of a cold horse barn as a drip barrier. Heated shops, warm livestock barns and produce storage need full insulation.

Will mice get into the insulation?

Rodents tunnel into loose or exposed insulation, especially at the wall base. We seal the bottom of the wall and cover low insulation with a tough liner or kick board so they cannot reach it.

Can animals chew spray foam?

Animals rub, kick and chew anything they can reach. Wherever livestock or horses can touch a wall, the insulation should sit behind a liner, kick board or panel strong enough to take it.

Should I insulate my hay barn?

Usually not fully. Hay barns need a roof that does not drip and good airflow. The bigger fire risk is hay baled too wet: extension safety guidance says bales above 20 percent moisture can heat up and spontaneously combust.

How do you insulate post-frame walls?

Post-frame walls have posts set far apart with horizontal girts, so there is no standard stud cavity. We use spray foam against the steel or insulation between girts and an added frame, a sealed vapor layer in heated buildings, and an interior liner to protect it.

Do I need a permit to insulate a barn?

It depends on your county. Codes based on the IBC list agricultural buildings and livestock shelters in Group U, and counties treat them in different ways. Turning a barn into a shop with an office, an event space or living quarters can bring very different rules.

How much does agricultural building insulation cost?

Agricultural building insulation cost depends most on whether the building needs a drip barrier or full insulation, then on size and height, liners and covers, removal of old insulation, clearing the building and travel to the site. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
