What a hospital insulation contractor does

A hospital insulation contractor installs and seals insulation in walls, ceilings and roofs so patient rooms are quieter and more comfortable, equipment noise stays contained and older wings stop losing heat. It works to the wall designs, firestop systems and infection controls that others set. Here is the split.

The short answer

How to insulate a hospital

Start from the patient: fill the walls between patient rooms and the walls facing corridors, and close the path over the top. Contain mechanical rooms, shafts and rooftop equipment over patient floors. Bring older wings up with roof and wall insulation. In rated walls, install only what the listed design allows. Do every step inside the ICRA class your infection prevention team sets, one room or zone at a time.

Infection control

ICRA classes I to IV, in plain words

Before any wall or ceiling is opened, your infection prevention team rates the work and the patients nearby and sets an ICRA class. The class decides the barriers, air control and cleanup. Insulation work in occupied areas often lands in Class III or IV. Here is what each class usually means on site.

  • 1Dust kept down
  • 2Vents and doors sealed
  • 3HVAC isolated
  • 4Critical barriers
  • 5Negative air, HEPA
  • 6Anteroom

Class III

Larger work, or work near higher risk patients. Typical for opening walls and ceilings on an occupied unit.

During the work

  • HVAC isolated in the work area
  • Critical barriers of sheetrock, plywood or plastic up before work starts
  • Negative air kept with HEPA-equipped units
  • Waste in covered containers and covered carts

When it is done

  • Barriers stay until safety and infection prevention inspect
  • Area cleaned by environmental services
  • Barriers removed carefully, then HEPA vacuum and wet mop
  • HVAC restored

Source: ASHE ICRA matrix of precautions for construction and renovation. Your hospital's own ICRA governs every job.

Hospitals are divided into fire and smoke compartments so patients can be protected in place. A rated wall is only rated if it is built exactly like its tested, listed design. That changes how insulation goes in. Compare the two.

Questions

Hospital insulation contractor FAQs

What does a hospital insulation contractor do?

A hospital insulation contractor like Grizzly installs and seals insulation in walls, ceilings and roofs so patient rooms are quieter and more comfortable and equipment noise stays contained. It works to the rated wall designs, firestop systems and infection controls set by the hospital and its design team.

Can insulation make patient rooms quieter at night?

It helps with noise that travels through walls and ceilings, like the next room, an ice machine alcove, a mechanical room or rooftop units. Noise through open doors, from alarms or from conversations at the nurse station needs operational changes and absorbing ceilings as well.

What is the HCAHPS quiet at night question?

The national HCAHPS patient survey asks: "During this hospital stay, how often was the area around your room quiet at night?" Patients answer never, sometimes, usually or always.

What ICRA class does insulation work need?

Your infection prevention team sets the class for each job based on the work and the patients nearby. Opening walls and ceilings on an occupied unit often lands in Class III or IV, which means critical barriers, negative air with HEPA units and inspection before barriers come down.

Who provides the barriers and negative air?

Usually the general contractor or the hospital's own team. We work inside those controls, keep our waste covered and leave the area ready for inspection and cleaning.

Can you insulate fire or smoke rated walls?

Yes, but only as the wall's listed design allows. Insulation type, thickness and density must match the tested design, and penetrations are firestopped by a firestop installer to listed systems. Your life safety team approves before walls close.

Do you install radiation or MRI shielding?

No. X-ray and CT shielding is decided by a qualified physicist and installed by a shielding contractor, and MRI shielding comes with the vendor's package. We insulate the walls around those rooms, coordinated with their drawings.

How do you work in a hospital that never closes?

In small phases: rooms released one or two at a time with bed management, procedure rooms on days with no cases, and public areas at night. Loud work is kept to agreed hours and every area is cleaned and inspected before it goes back into use.

How much does hospital insulation cost?

Hospital insulation cost depends on the ICRA class, how the work is phased, rated wall requirements, access to ceilings, shafts and roof, working hours and any removal of old insulation. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
