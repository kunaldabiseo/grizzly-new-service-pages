The short answer

How to insulate a brewery

Split the building by what each room does. Over the brewhouse, insulate and seal the roof deck so steam cannot condense on cold metal, and finish it with a washable surface. Insulate the cellar and cold rooms so they hold temperature through summer. Put sound insulation between production and the taproom, keep the mill room sealed and free of dust ledges, and tell your HVAC contractor how tight the rooms will be.

Condensation

Stopping drips over the brewhouse

Brewhouse drips are rarely a roof leak. Warm, wet air from boils and cleaning rises, touches a roof deck or wall that is colder than its dew point, and turns back into water. The cure is a warm, sealed surface the air cannot reach behind.

01

Seal to the deck

Closed-cell spray foam or a sealed assembly against the roof deck, so humid air never touches cold metal.

02

Close the penetrations

Vapor stacks, flues, pipes and conduit all pass through the roof and walls. Each one is sealed all the way around.

03

Finish you can wash

Foam covered with an approved coating or a washable liner panel, so cleaning does not soak the insulation.

04

Old insulation out

Wet, sagging or moldy insulation over a brewhouse comes out before new goes in.

Cellar and fermentation

Holding cellar temperature through summer and winter

Your glycol system controls the tanks. The room around them still matters: heat that gets through the roof and walls in summer becomes load on the room cooling, and cold in winter does the opposite. Slide the outside temperature to see which way the heat flows.

It is 30°F warmer outside. Every degree of that difference pushes heat through the roof and walls, and the room cooling has to take it back out. More insulation means less heat gets in.

Fermentation CO2

CO2, tighter rooms and who watches the air

Fermentation releases carbon dioxide, which is heavier than air and collects in low spots. OSHA's exposure limit for CO2 is 5,000 ppm over an 8-hour day. When we insulate and seal a cellar, the room gets tighter, so ventilation and CO2 monitoring have to be planned for that.

Grain and mill room

Mill room dust and the surfaces around it

Milled grain makes fine dust. NFPA 61 covers fire and dust explosion prevention in agricultural and food processing facilities, and your fire marshal decides how it applies to your mill. On our side, the walls and ceiling should not give that dust anywhere to settle.

Avoid

Exposed facings and ledges

Loose blanket facings, open purlins and fuzzy surfaces collect dust where nobody cleans.

Use

Smooth, sealed, covered

Insulation behind smooth, cleanable surfaces, with gaps and penetrations sealed so dust stays in the room.

Questions

Brewery insulation contractor FAQs

What does a brewery insulation contractor do?

A brewery insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals the building: roof decks and walls over the brewhouse and cellar, built-in-place cold rooms, wet-room finishes and sound insulation between production and the taproom. Glycol lines, tank jackets, prefab walk-in panels, ventilation and CO2 monitors are handled by other trades.

Why does the roof drip over our brewhouse?

Steam from boils and cleaning rises and meets a roof deck that is colder than its dew point, then condenses and drips. Insulation sealed tight to the deck keeps that surface warm and keeps humid air off the cold metal. Vapor stacks and exhaust that remove the steam come from your brewhouse supplier and HVAC contractor.

What insulation works in a wet brewery room?

Closed-cell spray foam or a sealed insulated assembly, covered by an approved coating or washable liner panel. It should not soak up water, and the finish has to stand up to hoses and cleaning.

Does insulation help keep the cellar cold?

Yes. Your glycol system controls the tanks, but the room still gains heat through the roof and walls in summer. Insulating and sealing the cellar cuts that heat, so the room cooling works less to hold its set point.

Is it safe to seal a fermentation room tighter?

It can be, as long as ventilation and CO2 monitoring are designed for it. Fermentation releases CO2, which is heavier than air and collects low, and OSHA's exposure limit is 5,000 ppm over 8 hours. We tell you and your HVAC contractor how tight the room will be before we start.

Can you make our taproom quieter?

We can reduce noise from production with sound batts in shared walls and above the ceiling, and by sealing the gaps sound travels through. Insulation reduces noise; it does not make a room soundproof, and glass walls still let sound through.

Do you insulate glycol lines or tanks?

No. Glycol and process pipe insulation is done by mechanical insulation contractors, and tank insulation comes from the tank maker. We insulate the building and rooms around them.

Can you work around our brew schedule?

Yes. We plan work for non-brew days or areas away from the brewhouse, cover tanks, kettles and packaging lines, keep spray away from open fermenters and clean up before your crew is back.

How much does brewery insulation cost?

Brewery insulation cost depends on which rooms are included, wet room details and finishes, working around tanks already in place, removal of wet insulation, sound work for the taproom and your schedule. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
