The short answer

How to insulate a medical office building

Start with privacy: fill the walls around exam, consult and behavioral health rooms with sound insulation and close the path over the top. Then contain noise from plumbing walls, dental and lab equipment and mechanical rooms. Insulate the roof and exterior walls so outside rooms stay comfortable. Do it all inside the dust and infection controls your facility sets, around patient hours.

Privacy, honestly

Exam room privacy: what walls can and cannot do

HIPAA does not require soundproof walls. HHS says the Privacy Rule does not require structural changes like soundproofing, but practices must take reasonable safeguards against conversations being overheard. Better walls are one of those safeguards. Pick a room to see what helps.

Exam room

Privacy need

History, symptoms and results are discussed at normal speaking volume, often with the door ajar. The wall to the next exam room and the corridor wall matter most.

What we do

Sound insulation in the walls to the next exam room and the corridor, the path over the top closed above the ceiling, and outlets and wall bases sealed.

Also needed

A door with seals, and sound masking in the corridor if your designer recommends it.

Source: HHS HIPAA FAQ 197. This is not legal advice; your privacy officer decides what is reasonable for your practice.

Clean, quiet work

Insulating while patients are still coming in

Opening a wall in a clinic releases dust. Many jurisdictions require an infection control risk assessment (ICRA) for healthcare construction through the FGI Guidelines, and that assessment sets the barriers and air controls for the job. We follow it. Pick your situation.

Notice

Work area. Keep this door closed.Barrier in place

Clinic open during the work

  • Work areas sealed off with the barriers your ICRA requires
  • Dusty work scheduled outside patient hours where possible
  • Walk-off mats and covered paths, cleaned every day
  • No work in rooms in use; rooms handed back clean

Your facility or infection prevention lead sets the ICRA class and the controls. Containment, negative air and HEPA units are usually provided by your general contractor.

Source: ASHE quick guide on infection control risk assessments.

Imaging rooms

X-ray, CT and MRI rooms: where we stop

Imaging rooms are the clearest case where insulation is not the answer. Radiation shielding and MRI shielding are specialist systems designed and checked by others. We work on the parts around them.

Not ours

X-ray and CT shielding

A qualified physicist decides the shielding, and a shielding contractor installs it. Insulation does nothing for radiation.

Not ours

MRI rooms

The MRI room shield is part of the equipment vendor's package. We keep away from it unless the vendor says otherwise.

Ours

The walls around them

Sound insulation in adjoining office and exam walls, and insulation in the exterior walls and roof above, coordinated with the shielding drawings.

Ours

Equipment room noise

Chillers and cooling for imaging equipment hum. Insulated walls and ceilings around those rooms keep it out of patient spaces.

Hum, gurgle and whine

Dental, lab and plumbing noise in patient rooms

Dental compressors and vacuum pumps, lab freezers, sterilizers and a restroom drain running behind the exam room wall all reach patients. Most of it can be contained with insulated walls and sealed penetrations.

Patient comfort

Cold exam rooms and patients in gowns

A patient in a thin gown notices a cold room far more than staff in scrubs do. Exam rooms on outside walls, corners and the top floor are usually the complaints. Some of it is insulation, some is HVAC.

Insulation helps

Outside wall and corner rooms

Uninsulated or poorly insulated exterior walls and gaps around windows. We insulate and seal what can be reached.

Insulation helps

Top floor rooms

Questions

Medical office building insulation contractor FAQs

Questions practices ask usNotes from the walk-through

What does a medical office building insulation contractor do?

A medical office building insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals the roof, exterior walls and the walls between exam, consult, office, lab and equipment rooms, so conversations stay private, equipment noise stays contained and patients stay comfortable. Shielding, doors, glass, HVAC and infection control planning come from other specialists.

Does HIPAA require soundproof exam rooms?

No. HHS says the HIPAA Privacy Rule does not require structural changes such as soundproofing. Practices must still take reasonable safeguards to limit conversations being overheard, and better walls are one option. Your privacy officer decides what is reasonable for your practice.

Will insulation make our exam rooms private?

It makes a big difference when the walls are filled, the path over the top of the wall is closed and outlets and gaps are sealed. Doors, glass, ducts and how loudly people talk set the final limit, so no room is truly soundproof.

Can you work while our clinic is open?

Yes. We work inside the barriers and controls your infection control risk assessment requires, schedule dusty work outside patient hours where possible, never work in rooms in use and hand rooms back clean.

What is an ICRA and who does it?

An infection control risk assessment sets the dust and air controls for construction in a healthcare space. Many jurisdictions require one through the FGI Guidelines. Your facility or infection prevention lead sets it, and we follow it.

Can you insulate X-ray or MRI rooms?

Radiation and MRI shielding are specialist systems that insulation does not replace. A physicist and shielding contractor handle X-ray rooms, and the equipment vendor handles MRI. We insulate the walls around them, coordinated with their drawings.

How do you quiet a dental compressor or vacuum pump room?

We insulate the walls and ceiling of the equipment room and seal the penetrations, while keeping the ventilation the equipment needs. Vibration mounts and quieter equipment come from your equipment supplier.

Who pays for insulation in a multi-tenant medical office building?

It depends on the lease. Usually the building owner handles the roof, exterior walls and walls between suites, and each practice handles the walls inside its own suite. We work for either and follow the building's rules.

How much does medical office building insulation cost?

Medical office building insulation cost depends on the number of rooms, the privacy level, whether walls are open or finished, the infection controls required, working hours and whether the roof and exterior are included. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
