The short answer

How to insulate an industrial plant building

Start with what the air inside the building is doing to it: chemicals, moisture, heat and dust. Choose insulation, facings and fasteners that survive that air. Insulate enough to keep pump and pipe rooms above freezing on the coldest nights, seal the building so wet or corrosive air stays where it belongs, and do every step inside the plant's permit and safety system.

Materials that survive

Corrosion, moisture and the right materials

In harsh plants the insulation rarely fails first. The facing tears, the fasteners rust or the vapor layer opens up, and then the insulation gets wet. So we choose the whole assembly for the exposure.

  1. A
    Facings

    Durable, cleanable facings or liner panels where air is wet or dirty, instead of paper or thin vinyl that tears.

  2. B
    Fasteners and trims

    Coated or stainless fasteners where corrosive air would eat plain steel. We pick them with your engineer.

  3. C
    Closed-cell foam

    Seals air and moisture out of the assembly. Needs a thermal barrier or approved coating in occupied space, and the right coating for chemical exposure.

  4. D
    Continuous vapor layer

    In wet buildings the vapor layer is the whole game. Every seam and penetration is closed.

Plant safety

Working inside your plant's safety program

Where a plant runs a process covered by OSHA's process safety management rule, contractors have to be trained in the plant's safe work practices, told about "known potential fire, explosion, or toxic release hazards" and follow "safety rules of the facility". Most industrial plants run similar programs even when the rule does not apply.

People spaces

Control rooms, offices and break rooms on site

The people who run a plant spend their shifts in control rooms, labs, offices and break rooms that sit right next to loud, hot or dusty process areas. These rooms are often where insulation makes the biggest daily difference.

Sound

Mineral wool or sound batts in shared walls and above ceilings cut down noise from pumps, blowers and crushers. It reduces noise; it does not make a room soundproof.

Comfort

Insulated walls and ceilings keep control rooms steady when the process side is hot in summer or freezing in winter.

Dust and air

Sealed walls and ceilings keep process dust and odors out of rooms where people eat and work, alongside the HVAC filtration.

Questions

Industrial plant insulation contractor FAQs

What does an industrial plant insulation contractor do?

An industrial plant insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates the buildings at a plant: roofs, walls, pump houses, control rooms and storage buildings, with materials chosen for corrosive, wet, hot or dusty air. Pipe, tank, boiler and equipment insulation is process insulation, done by mechanical insulation specialists.

Do you insulate pipes, tanks or boilers?

No. Process insulation on pipes, valves, tanks, boilers and equipment, and heat tracing, is specialist mechanical insulation work. We handle the building envelope around the process.

How do you stop a pump house from freezing?

By cutting the heat the building loses so its heater can keep up on the coldest night: insulating walls and roof, sealing air leaks and closing roof-to-wall gaps. Your mechanical engineer sizes the heater; the insulation decides how big it has to be.

What insulation works in a wastewater plant?

Closed-cell foam or insulation behind durable liner panels, sealed to a continuous vapor layer, with corrosion-rated fasteners and coatings. Hydrogen sulfide in wastewater buildings attacks plain steel and thin facings, so the whole assembly is chosen for that exposure with your engineer.

Can you work under our plant's safety program?

Yes. We bring training records, safety data sheets, insurance and job hazard analyses, complete your orientation, and follow your hot work, confined space, lockout and entry rules. Where OSHA's process safety management rule applies, contractors must be trained in the plant's safe work practices and follow its safety rules.

Can you work during a plant outage?

Yes. We stage materials and plan crew size around the outage window you give us, coordinate with the mechanical and electrical crews, and walk the punch list with your team before restart.

Is spray foam allowed near hot work or furnaces?

Exposed foam has to be kept away from hot work and covered by a thermal barrier in occupied space. Near furnaces and constant hot work we usually recommend non-combustible insulation such as mineral wool instead.

Why does insulation fail early in industrial buildings?

Usually the facing tears, the fasteners corrode or the vapor layer opens up, and then the insulation gets wet. That is why we choose facings, fasteners and seals for the exposure, not just the insulation.

How much does industrial plant insulation cost?

Industrial plant insulation cost depends on the safety program, the exposure and materials it calls for, removal of damaged insulation, access around equipment, work windows and the number of buildings. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
