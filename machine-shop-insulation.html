The short answer

How to insulate a machine shop

Insulate and seal the roof first, because that is where most drips and summer heat come from. Then close the air leaks at doors and wall edges, insulate the walls, and use non-combustible insulation wherever welding and grinding happen. Finish with covered, wipeable surfaces so oil mist and chips do not settle into the insulation, and give the office and inspection room their own insulated walls.

Heat and tolerance

Why shop temperature swings show up in your parts

Metal grows as it warms. The international reference temperature for measuring parts is 20°C, which is 68°F (ISO 1). A shop that swings through the day moves your parts, fixtures and gauges with it. Set up a part like yours and see how much of the tolerance the swing eats.

That uses 98% of a ±0.001 in tolerance before any machining error.

Uses typical expansion rates per °F: steel about 6.5, stainless 304 about 9.6, aluminum about 13, cast iron about 5.8 and brass about 11.3 millionths of an inch per inch. Real parts, machines and gauges move at different rates and speeds, so treat this as a feel for the size of the effect.

Honest part: insulation and air sealing slow how fast a metal building swings and make it far easier for your heating and cooling to hold a steady number. The HVAC holds the setpoint. Shops chasing tight tolerances also let parts and gauges soak to the same temperature before measuring.

Rust and condensation

Rust and condensation on machines, tooling and stock

Steel sweats whenever its surface is colder than the dew point of the air around it. In a machine shop that moisture comes from three places, and only one of them is fixed by insulation alone. Here is which is which.

  1. From the roof
    Insulation fixes this

    Warm, moist shop air rises and meets a cold metal deck. It condenses, runs along purlins and drips onto machines, parts and electrical cabinets. Insulation sealed tight to the deck keeps that surface warm enough to stay dry.

  2. From the air
    Shared job

    Humid days, an open door, and water-based coolant evaporating all add moisture. A sealed envelope keeps outside air out and makes the room easier to control. Dehumidifiers and mist collectors belong to your HVAC contractor and mist collector supplier.

  3. From cold stock
    Shop practice

    Bar and plate delivered on a freezing morning is colder than the shop air, so it sweats when it comes inside. Insulation cannot change that. Storing stock indoors and letting it warm up before it goes in the machine does.

Welding and hot work

Welding, hot work and spray foam in a machine shop

OSHA's welding rule says combustibles should be moved "at least 35 feet" from hot work or protected, and it bars welding on a metal wall, ceiling or roof "having a combustible covering". Foam on the back of a metal wall is a combustible covering. Answer three questions to see what belongs where in your shop.

Oil mist and chips

Oil mist, chips and surfaces you can wipe down

Coolant and oil mist settles on everything, and loose facings catch it like a filter. Lower walls take hits from forklifts and bar stock. The surface over the insulation matters as much as the insulation. Pick a finish to compare.

Faced blanket insulation

Fiberglass blanket with a vinyl or reinforced facing, common in metal buildings. Affordable, but thin facings tear, sag and collect oil mist, and gaps at seams let air through.

Compressor room

Compressor rooms: heat, noise and airflow

According to the Compressed Air Challenge, as much as 80 to 93 percent of the electricity an industrial air compressor uses turns into heat. That heat and the noise are why the compressor room deserves its own plan.

Where the compressor's electricity goes

Noise out of the office

Insulated walls and ceilings with mineral wool or sound batts cut the noise reaching the office and break room. They reduce noise; they do not make the room silent.

Do not choke the airflow

A compressor needs cooling air. We insulate around the openings your compressor dealer or HVAC contractor specifies, never over them.

Heat you can reuse

Ducting that heat into the shop in winter is a job for your HVAC contractor or compressor dealer. A well insulated shop holds on to it longer.

Rooms inside the shop

Inspection rooms, CMM rooms and the office

The room where you measure parts should not follow the shop's temperature around. Most shops build it as a box inside the building, with its own insulated walls and ceiling and its own climate control.

  • Insulated walls and ceilingSeparates the room from the shop's swings, heat from the machines and the cold overhead door.
  • Sealed door and penetrationsKeeps shop air, mist and dust out of the room where gauges and the CMM live.
  • Sound for the officeThe same walls cut machine noise for the people doing quotes and programming.
  • HVAC sets the numberHolding a tight temperature band is your HVAC or lab specialist's design. We build the envelope that lets it hold.

Questions

Machine shop insulation contractor FAQs

What does a machine shop insulation contractor do?

A machine shop insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals the shop building: the roof, walls, overhead door areas, compressor room, office and inspection room. Heating, cooling, dehumidifiers and mist collectors are installed by other trades, and we work alongside them.

How do I stop my shop roof from dripping on the machines?

Insulate the roof deck and seal it tight so warm, moist shop air cannot reach the cold metal. Closed-cell spray foam or a sealed liner system both work. Patching drips with loose blankets usually moves the problem a few feet.

Will insulation keep my machines and stock from rusting?

It stops rust caused by roof drips and helps by sealing out humid outside air. It cannot stop cold stock from sweating when it comes inside, and humidity from coolant may still need a dehumidifier or mist collector. We tell you which part is which on the walk-through.

Does insulation help hold tolerances?

Indirectly. Metal grows as it warms, and a poorly insulated metal shop swings through the day. Insulation and air sealing slow that swing and let your heating and cooling hold a steady temperature. The HVAC sets the number.

Is spray foam safe near welding?

Foam must be covered by an approved thermal barrier in occupied space and kept away from hot work. OSHA bars welding on a metal wall or roof with a combustible covering, so we use non-combustible mineral wool in weld areas and on any wall you weld to.

Can you insulate while we keep running?

Yes. We wrap machines, controls and stock before any spray or cutting, work one bay at a time so the rest of the shop keeps cutting, and can work evenings or weekends.

What is the best insulation for a metal machine shop?

It depends on the zone. Spray foam or a liner system on the roof, covered wall insulation with metal liner panel where forklifts and bar stock hit, mineral wool near welding, and sound batts around the compressor room and office.

Should the inspection room be insulated separately?

Yes. Most shops build the QC or CMM room as an insulated box inside the building, with its own climate control, so it does not follow the shop's temperature swings.

How much does machine shop insulation cost?

Machine shop insulation cost depends on roof and wall area and height, cleaning oily surfaces, protecting machines, hot work zones, removal of old insulation and the hours we can work. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
