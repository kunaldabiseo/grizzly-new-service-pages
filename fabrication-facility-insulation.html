The short answer

How to insulate a fabrication facility

Work from the top down. Insulate and seal the roof deck, where most heat escapes in winter and pours in during summer. Plan the crane zone so insulation clears the runway and the crane can be locked out while we work. Use non-combustible mineral wool on walls near welding and cutting, protect lower walls from impact, and seal the big doors and wall bases where cold air rushes in.

Hot work

Welding, cutting and insulation: plan by distance

OSHA's welding and cutting rule says combustibles should be moved "at least 35 feet" from the work or protected, and it bars welding on a metal wall or roof "having a combustible covering". So in a fab shop, distance from the work decides the insulation. Set your closest station.

Flash rust and rework

Roof drips, flash rust and parts waiting for paint

Blasted or cleaned steel waiting for paint rusts fast when water drips on it. In winter, warm, moist shop air condenses on a cold, bare roof deck and falls right onto the work. Here is where that moisture hurts along the line.

  1. Lower risk

    Cut

    Plate and shapes from the yard can sweat when they come inside cold. That is a storage habit, not an insulation fix.

  2. Some risk

    Fit and weld

    Drips on fitted parts mean drying time and cleanup before welding.

  3. High risk

    Blast and prep

    Freshly prepped steel shows flash rust quickly when it gets wet. A drip can mean doing the prep again.

  4. High risk

    Paint and coat

    Moisture on the surface under paint is a coating problem waiting to happen. Your coating supplier sets the surface rules.

Our part: insulation sealed tight to the roof deck keeps its underside warm enough to stay dry. Seal the roof-to-wall edges too, or moist air finds the cold spots there instead.

Paint and powder coat

Paint booths, powder coat and cure ovens

Spray finishing with flammable materials is covered by NFPA 33 and OSHA's spray finishing rule. The booth, its exhaust and the cure oven are engineered equipment. Our job is the building around them.

  • Ours

    What we do

    Insulate and seal the walls and roof around the finishing area, and the rooms next to it, with materials your fire marshal accepts for that space.

  • Not ours

    What we leave alone

    Booth panels, exhaust ducts, make-up air units and oven insulation stay with the equipment maker and your HVAC contractor.

  • Why

    Why it still matters

    Ovens and booths add heat and pull large amounts of air. A tight, insulated building around them is easier to keep at the temperature your coatings need.

See 29 CFR 1910.107, spray finishing and NFPA 33.

Truck doors and drive-through bays

Big doors, drafts and drive-through bays

Steel comes in and finished work goes out on trucks and trailers, so the doors are big and open often. The door itself is the door company's job. The wall and framing around it, and the cold air that sneaks past, are ours.

    Fabrication facility insulation contractor FAQs

    What does a fabrication facility insulation contractor do?

    A fabrication facility insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals the building shell of a steel or sheet metal fab shop: the roof deck, walls, door framing, wall bases and the rooms inside. Fume extraction, cranes, paint booths, heaters and doors are installed by other trades.

    What insulation is safe near welding and cutting?

    Non-combustible mineral wool behind a durable cover. OSHA says combustibles should be moved at least 35 feet from welding and cutting or protected, and bars welding on a metal wall or roof with a combustible covering, so foam is kept away from hot work and never put on a wall you weld to.

    Can you insulate above an overhead crane?

    Yes. We check clearances to the runway, bridge and lights, choose a method that does not hang into the crane path, and work bay by bay with the crane locked out under your procedure.

    Why does my fab shop roof drip in winter?

    Warm, moist shop air meets the cold underside of a bare or poorly sealed metal roof and condenses. Insulation sealed tight to the deck keeps that surface warm enough to stay dry. On prepped steel waiting for paint, those drips mean flash rust and rework.

    Do you insulate paint booths or cure ovens?

    No. The booth, its exhaust and the oven are engineered equipment from their makers. We insulate and seal the building and rooms around the finishing area, with materials your fire marshal accepts for that space.

    Should we insulate the roof from inside or with a new roof?

    If the roof is being replaced anyway, insulation is usually added with the new roof by the roofing contractor, and we handle the walls and any interior removal. If not, spray foam under the deck or a liner system under the purlins is the usual path.

    Can you work while we keep fabricating?

    Yes. We schedule bays with your production lead, follow your crane lockout and hot work rules, keep welding away from the spray area while foam goes on, and can work nights, weekends or during a shutdown.

    What happens to the old sooty insulation?

    Facings that are only dirty can sometimes stay. Torn, sagging or debris-dropping insulation is removed and bagged before new insulation goes in.

    How much does fabrication facility insulation cost?

    Fabrication facility insulation cost depends on area and eave height, cranes and lockout windows, hot work zones, removal of old insulation, protecting parts and machines, and your schedule. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
