What a cannabis grow facility insulation contractor does

A cannabis grow facility insulation contractor builds the envelope: the insulated, sealed shell around every room. What happens inside the room, from lights to dehumidifiers to CO2, belongs to other trades. Think of it as a box inside a box.

The envelopeGrizzly

  • Roof deck and exterior wall insulation
  • Insulated room walls and ceilings

  • Removal of wet or moldy insulation
  • Offices, trim rooms and break rooms

Inside the roomOther trades

  • CO2 enrichment and monitoring
  • Carbon filters and odor control
  • Irrigation, security and cameras

The short answer

How to insulate a cannabis grow facility

Treat every grow room as a wet room. Use insulation that does not soak up water, usually closed-cell spray foam or insulated panels, and keep a continuous vapor and air seal on the warm, wet side. Seal every seam and penetration so the room is tight for air, odor, CO2 and light. Insulate between rooms that run at different temperatures, and cover foam with a cleanable, approved finish.

  1. Wet room thinking

    Every grow room is humid all day, every day.

  2. Materials that stay dry

    Closed-cell foam or panels, not materials that wick water.

  3. One continuous seal

    Vapor, air and light, closed at every seam.

  4. Room from room

    Insulate walls between rooms that run differently.

  5. Clean finish

    A wipeable, approved cover over the insulation.

Mold and materials

Mold, wet walls and choosing grow room insulation

Mold in a grow room usually starts inside the wall, where moist room air reaches insulation that holds water. The fix is a material that stays dry and a seal with no gaps. Here is how the usual options compare in a wet room.

  • Good fit

    Closed-cell spray foam

    Resists water and vapor, seals air and light in one pass. Needs an approved thermal barrier or coating in occupied rooms.

  • Good fit

    Insulated metal panels

    Cleanable, tight and common in purpose-built grows. Often installed by a panel contractor; we insulate and seal around them.

  • Use with care

    Open-cell spray foam

    Lets vapor through and can hold water. We rarely recommend it on the wet side of a grow room.

  • Risky

    Fiberglass batts behind board

    Fine in dry rooms. Behind a grow room wall, one gap in the vapor layer can leave wet insulation hidden from view.

Odor and CO2

Keeping odor and CO2 where they belong

Air that leaks out of a grow room takes odor with it, and CO2 you paid for goes with it too. Filters and controls handle the air you move on purpose. Air sealing handles the air you never meant to lose.

Filter

Scented, CO2-rich air slips out at the wall top, penetrations and the door frame, around your filters.

Illustrative

Odor

Gaps at the roof deck, wall tops and penetrations let scented air reach offices, neighbors and the street. Sealing them gives your carbon filters a fair chance.

CO2

Enriched rooms only hold their CO2 level if the room is tight. Every leak means more CO2 to replace. Monitors and controls belong to your HVAC or CO2 supplier.

Tight rooms, safe rooms

A tighter room changes how it is ventilated. Your mechanical engineer sets the airflow and safety monitoring; we build the room to the tightness they design for.

Heat and cooling

Grow lights, roof heat and cooling costs

Grow rooms already make a lot of heat from lights and equipment, so in many grows the cooling side works harder than the heating side. A hot summer roof and leaky walls add to that load. Toggle the roof to see where the extra heat comes from.

Extraction and processing

Extraction rooms and fire code

NFPA 1, the fire code, has a chapter for marijuana growing, processing and extraction facilities. Extraction rooms that use flammable solvents are engineered spaces with their own equipment, ventilation and approvals. Here is where our work starts and stops.

  • We do

    Insulate and seal the building and the rooms around extraction, with materials your fire marshal accepts for that space.

  • We do not

    Design or build extraction booths, hazardous location ventilation or gas detection. Those come from the equipment maker and your engineer.

  • We ask first

    Before any spray foam goes near an extraction or solvent storage room, we get the approved materials and details in writing.

Questions

Cannabis grow facility insulation contractor FAQs

What does a cannabis grow facility insulation contractor do?

A cannabis grow facility insulation contractor like Grizzly insulates and seals the envelope of a grow building: the roof deck, exterior walls and the walls and ceilings of each grow room, sealed for vapor, air and light. HVAC, dehumidification, lighting, CO2, odor filtration and security are installed by other trades.

What is the best insulation for a grow room?

Usually closed-cell spray foam or insulated metal panels, because they do not soak up water and help form a continuous vapor and air seal. Exposed foam in occupied rooms needs an approved thermal barrier or coating. Open-cell foam and fiberglass batts are riskier on the wet side of a grow room.

Why do grow rooms get mold in the walls?

Plants release most of the water they take up into the air. If that humid air reaches insulation through a gap in the vapor seal, it condenses on colder surfaces inside the wall and the insulation stays wet. A continuous seal and insulation that does not hold water prevent it.

Can you make a flower room light-tight?

Yes. Light gets in through the same gaps as air: the top of the wall at the roof deck, panel seams, duct and pipe penetrations, electrical boxes, door frames and the wall base. We seal each of them. Door seals and sweeps come from the door supplier.

Does insulation help with odor control?

Air sealing does. Odor leaves through the same gaps as air, so a sealed room lets your carbon filters handle the air you move on purpose. Filtration and odor control equipment come from your HVAC or odor control supplier.

Can you work on a licensed cannabis premises?

Yes. Colorado rules require every visitor to a Limited Access Area, including contractors, to get a visitor badge from licensed staff, and the business keeps a visitor log. We send crew names ahead, wear badges, follow your escort rules and take no photos of plants or product.

Can you convert a warehouse into a grow facility?

We handle the insulation side of a conversion: surveying the shell, insulating the roof deck or building insulated ceilings, insulating room walls and sealing everything for air, vapor and light. Room layout, HVAC and electrical come from your designer and other trades.

Do you insulate extraction rooms?

We insulate and seal the building and rooms around extraction with materials your fire marshal accepts. Extraction booths, hazardous location ventilation and gas detection are designed and built by the equipment maker and your engineer.

How much does cannabis grow facility insulation cost?

Cannabis grow facility insulation cost depends on the number of rooms and their area, how tight each room needs to be, the materials, removal of wet or moldy insulation, working around live rooms and your site access rules. We quote from a free walk-through with an itemized scope letter.
